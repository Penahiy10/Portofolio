Population Growth: Indonesia in Global Context

Python · pandas · matplotlib

Compared Indonesia's population growth against Saudi Arabia and Malaysia — two countries relevant to the Hajj/Umrah sector — using World Bank data from 1960 to 2024.

Compound annual growth rate, 1960–2024 (World Bank population data).

Indonesia's population nearly tripled since 1960 (88.3M → 283.5M), but its annual growth rate is the lowest of the three — expected, given how large its 1960 base already was. Saudi Arabia grew fastest by far (14.5×), likely a mix of natural growth and large-scale labor migration, though the dataset alone can't separate the two.

Limitation — total-population figures only; births, deaths, and migration aren't broken out, so the exact cause of the growth gap can't be confirmed from this data alone. No public pilgrim-volume dataset was found, so country choice was based on sector relevance rather than direct pilgrim numbers.

Energy Transition: Indonesia vs. Vietnam

Python · pandas

Analyzed a global energy dataset spanning 1900–2024 to compare renewable-energy adoption between two similarly-developing Southeast Asian economies, then widened the lens to test whether richer countries generally lean more renewable.

23,000+rows analyzed
124years of data (1900–2024)
79countries in the GDP correlation
Question asked — does GDP per capita predict a country's share of renewable energy? The correlation across 79 countries turned out weaker than the "richer = greener" assumption suggests — policy choice, not wealth alone, drives the gap between Indonesia and Vietnam.
